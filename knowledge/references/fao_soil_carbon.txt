Title: FAO - Soil Organic Carbon: The Hidden Potential (2017), summary notes
Soil organic carbon (SOC) is a central indicator of soil health, influencing water holding
capacity, nutrient cycling, and microbial habitat. Depleted SOC (below roughly 1% in many
cropland soils) is associated with reduced structural stability, lower infiltration, and
weaker resilience to drought. Practices that return organic matter to soil - cover cropping,
reduced tillage, organic amendments, and diversified rotations - have been repeatedly shown
to rebuild SOC over multi-year timescales, with legume cover crops among the fastest-acting
interventions due to nitrogen fixation supporting rapid biomass turnover.
