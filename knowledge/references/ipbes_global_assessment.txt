Title: IPBES Global Assessment on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (2019), summary notes
Land-use change, particularly conversion to intensive monoculture, is identified as the
single largest driver of terrestrial biodiversity loss globally. Habitat fragmentation
reduces connectivity between remaining natural patches, constraining species movement and
gene flow. Landscape features such as hedgerows, field margins, and riparian buffers can
partially restore connectivity even within otherwise intensive agricultural landscapes,
supporting pollinators and natural pest predators.
